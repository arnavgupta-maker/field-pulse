Generative AI and Firm Values

This paper operationalizes task-exposure frameworks like *GPTs are GPTs* at the firm level, demonstrating that equity markets immediately priced workforce exposure to Generative AI and that this effect is amplified by firm data assets. Crucially for labor economics, it extends the core-versus-supplemental task debate (building on Autor, Levy, & Murnane) by using early firm labor demand and profitability to provide direct empirical evidence of labor-technology *substitution* rather than pure augmentation.

From AI Readiness to Productivity: The Role of Innovation Capacity in Europe's Digital Transformation

While your core list focuses on micro-level task exposure and direct labor market impacts (e.g., *GPTs are GPTs*, *Generative AI at Work*), this paper offers a macroeconomic counterpoint by showing that "AI readiness" fails to yield aggregate productivity gains without business R&D and complementary innovation capacity. It highlights a key aggregate "translation gap," suggesting that the firm-level productivity potential of generative AI will not automatically scale into national-level economic growth without structural innovation investments.

Governing cognitive labor delegated to AI in scholarly writing and medical education

This paper is largely tangential to core empirical labor economics, but it offers useful context on the institutional guardrails governing the delegation of non-routine cognitive tasks to GenAI.
