Evaluating the Potential Economic Impact of Artificial Intelligence on Professions: A Refined Approach Using Ensemble Embeddings

This paper advances the methodology of *GPTs are GPTs* by replacing subjective, manual task-matching with an objective, deep-learning ensemble embedding framework to quantify occupational exposure to AI. In doing so, it provides robust algorithmic evidence that directly challenges the classic routine-biased technological change paradigm established by Autor, Levy, and Murnane (2003), confirming that AI uniquely targets high-wage, highly educated cognitive professions.

AI-MEDIATED PLATFORM WORK: MEANINGFULNESS UNDER ALGORITHMIC MANAGEMENT

This conceptual paper bridges the task-based labor framework used in your core reading list (e.g., Autor et al. and Acemoglu & Restrepo) with organizational sociology by modeling how simultaneous AI substitution and augmentation alter non-monetary work quality ("meaningfulness") under algorithmic control. However, because it is a purely theoretical framework focused on worker psychology and platform governance rather than empirical estimations of wages, employment, or firm productivity, it represents a peripheral, interdisciplinary extension rather than a core labor economics text.
